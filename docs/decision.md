## 1. THE PROBLEM, IN ONE SENTENCE, WITH ITS CITATIONS

When fund data changes accounting systems, accountants need to resolve and recheck the decisions behind each number, because a completed upload file can still contain accounting treatments whose approval is not recorded. [Call 1:57-66](../.claude/md_references/call-1-nav-workflow-review.md:57); reference workbook, `Mapping Gaps!A2:G3` and `Upload Template (VERIFIED v4c)!L3024:L3034`.

The decision is to build **migration sign-off** for the fund accountant receiving the migration: an output is approved against specific mapping versions, and a subsequent mapping change invalidates the affected approval. This is the one product for this weekend.

## 3. WHAT WE BUILD, AS A SINGLE USER STORY

**As the fund accountant receiving a migration, I want to resolve the outstanding accounting decisions and see exactly what a correction changes, so I can release a reviewed loader and explain any amount without reopening multiple workbooks.**

One session, five steps. The accountant operates it; a non-technical manager should understand its outcome from labels such as "Needs a decision", "Changed since approval" and "Ready to export". No network graph, chat landing page or editable wall of 43 columns.

1. **MUST: upload and establish the scope.** Data: GL `Investor-Level GL`; reference `LE Mapping`, `Entity Listing`, `CoA Mapping`, `Investor Mapping`, `Deal Mapping`, `Batch Preference`, `Corvus CoA`, `Deals List`, `Investors List`, `Suppliers List` and `Mapping Gaps`; the Phase I sample's `Sheet1` header defines the destination layout. Screen: **Migration overview**, showing the files, entity scope, period and decisions required. Done: retain each original file and physical row identity; parse all 33,902 GL rows; show scope discrepancies without fuzzy-renaming entities. For the benchmark, freeze the reference's 52-entity population as an explicitly reference-derived scope manifest; do not infer scope anew from whichever rows the generator happens to produce. **p50: 2 person-hours. p90: 4**, driven by mixed headers, duplicate source column names and entity aliases; both source `GL Date` columns and both `Static Date` columns must remain distinguishable by position.

2. **MUST: prepare candidate postings and surface decisions.** Data: GL `Investor-Level GL` plus the four mapping sheets, `Batch Preference`, `Corvus CoA` and the three master lists. Screen: **Review queue**, grouped by accounting question, with affected entities, row count and gross amounts shown separately by currency. Done: apply explicit mappings for supported cases, retain mapping-row references and versions, and show the two documented gaps as decisions rather than silently borrowing the reference's answers. Repeated mapping rows that resolve to the same target ID do not multiply postings; conflicting targets or unsupported rules block their affected batches. Existing crosswalks remain read-only in v1; the editor only creates or revises the two gap decisions. **p50: 4 person-hours. p90: 8**, driven by debit/credit-dependent transaction types, batch priorities and scope/zero-row treatment. Complete regeneration of every reference field is **SHOULD**; a visible blocked case is preferable to an invented default. The reference upload's values and `Movements Rec` are evaluation inputs only, never generator inputs.

3. **MUST: resolve a real decision and record why.** Data: reference `Mapping Gaps!A2:G3`, the affected GL rows and valid targets in `Corvus CoA`; current imported crosswalk versions provide context. Screen: **Decision drawer**, initially opened on the 11 administration-fee rows. Done: display the conflicting source account/type, a human-selected target treatment, the decision author, reason and affected source rows; approving records an immutable decision version. The supplied spelling `Expense: Adminstration fees` is a valid target label at `Corvus CoA!F911`, not a typo to fix automatically. The second gap's near-zero net does not make its eight nonzero postings disposable. The demo reviewer acts explicitly in a simulated accountant role; their approval is not attributed to the original administrator. **p50: 3 person-hours. p90: 5**, driven by approval persistence and unclear accounting authority. AI-written explanations are **CUT-FIRST**; they are not needed to resolve or record a decision.

4. **MUST: revise a decision and recheck the release.** Data: the new decision version, all source rows and batch peers depending on it, `Batch Preference`, and held-out `Movements Rec`/`Upload Template (VERIFIED v4c)` for comparison. Screen: **Release check**, showing previous and current treatment, changed amounts by currency and which approvals are stale. Done: make one explicitly labelled demonstration change to an approved gap mapping; identify exactly the dependent postings, revoke the affected batch release approvals and prevent a stale version from being downloaded as approved. Unrelated approvals remain intact. Reconfirm the intended treatment and approve the new version before release; reverting a value does not resurrect the old approval automatically. Withhold whole affected batches, not isolated debit/credit legs; a file with unresolved blockers stays a review draft. **p50: 3 person-hours. p90: 6**, driven by batch-level dependencies, stale-state enforcement and reconciliation mismatches. Live accounting-system posting and destination receipts are **CUT-FIRST**; the UI says "Exported; destination not checked", never "Migration accepted".

5. **MUST: export, then explain one amount.** Data: the generated upload rows, their retained GL physical row references, all mapping versions used and the recorded decisions/approvals. Screen: **Amount evidence drawer**, opened by clicking an amount in the export preview. Done: download a loader for fully reviewed batches with its explicit scope, then click an amount and see its contributing source rows, mapped investor/entity, classification rule, decision reason and current approval. Show the prior version as superseded, keep identical source-row occurrences separate and retain the same evidence after refresh. **p50: 2 person-hours. p90: 4**, driven by export typing, provenance persistence and large-row rendering. Animated graph views and general mapping editors are **CUT-FIRST**.

Reserve another **5 person-hours** for UI finish, meaningful state-transition and reconciliation checks, a clean-checkout one-command run, README and demo rehearsal: **19 person-hours**, planned within **12 elapsed hours for two people**. One person owns file interpretation, generation and comparison; the other owns review UI, durable decisions and release enforcement, with the row/version contract agreed before they separate. These are work assignments for the two humans, not a request to spawn agents in this turn.

## 4. THE DEMO NUMBER

**The single video claim is: "All 19 of the 19 postings affected by the two documented mapping gaps are identified and prevented from approved export until their accounting decisions are recorded."** This is a target for the product test, not a completed implementation result. It measures the chosen sign-off problem; reproducing every row of a file containing unrecorded approvals would not demonstrate that control.

Compute `N / 19`, where a posting counts only if the product identifies its exact source occurrence, links its candidate output to the applicable gap decision, withholds the affected batch before approval, and invalidates that batch's approval when the mapping version changes. Test the actual release/download path, not just the screen label. An unrelated, already-reviewed batch is the negative control: it must not be labelled dependent on the edited mapping or lose its own approval. The number of withheld batch peers may exceed 19; do not call all those peers direct mapping-gap matches.

The denominator comes from the files, not from what the implementation finds. `Mapping Gaps` row 2 corresponds to 11 source rows in the benchmark scope and reference upload rows 3024 through 3034. ## 5. THE 60-SECOND PITCH AND THE THREE HARD QUESTIONS

Opening narration, about 145 words. Read the number from the tested application at recording time; the final sentence makes the open commercial question explicit.

> "What I care about is the count of turns. That is the drag on my time." That is a fund manager describing repeated reviews of his administrator's work.
>
> We found a concrete version of that problem in the migration dataset: nineteen postings have mapping questions with no approval recorded, but their treatments already appear in the verified upload.
>
> Files can move while the reasoning behind them remains unresolved. We built a sign-off workspace for the accountant receiving that migration. It connects each decision to the postings it changes and records who approved it and why.
>
> In this run, we protect [N] of those nineteen postings from approved export until their decisions are recorded. Watch me change an approved mapping: its dependent approval expires. Click this amount and the source rows and decision history are there.
>
> The open question is whether this earns repeat use after the migration ends.

**1. "Why would anyone pay for this instead of doing it in Excel once?"**

For a small one-off migration with a disciplined workbook, they may not; we have no willingness-to-pay evidence. The proposed paid job is avoiding repeated manual impact checks and sign-off reconstruction across revisions, and we would test that against one accountant's actual rework before asserting a recurring business.

**2. "So it is lineage, and why could eFront or Accelex not do this?"**

Lineage is the dependency record; the demonstrated behaviour is that a mapping change revokes only the affected release approvals and blocks stale output. The existing research does not establish that those vendors lack this, so our claim is a working, file-based decision-control workflow on this dataset, not unique technology.

**3. "Are you just copying the verified loader, and does matching it prove the accounting is right?"**

The generator reads the source, supplied mapping/master sheets and explicit demo-reviewer decisions; the reference upload and reconciliation are reserved for comparison, with the benchmark entity scope disclosed as reference-derived. Matching shows fidelity to that artefact, not original administrator approval or destination acceptance, which is why we separately demonstrate the missing-decision gate and never claim a live migration was accepted.

**Ask the Ylookup engineer before building:** For the 11 administration-fee postings in `Mapping Gaps` row 2 that already appear in the verified loader, what exact decision still needed sign-off before the migration could be accepted?
